# BDR practice case: Cedar & Loom

This is a fictional portfolio exercise. Cedar & Loom, its needs, and every interaction below are invented. No prospect was contacted, no message was sent, and no sales result is claimed.

## Research before outreach

**Available evidence:** the app's synthetic brief says Cedar & Loom is a home-and-living business with 11–50 people and an order-status support scenario. Its example.com link is a placeholder, not an independent source.

**Hypothesis to test:** repeated order-status questions may take time away from other customer work. A clear self-service answer and sensible human handoff might help. The brief does not establish request volume, budget, current tools, or interest in a product.

## Three discovery questions

1. How do customers currently check an order, and where do they get stuck?
2. How often do order-status questions arrive, and who handles them?
3. What would a useful improvement look like, and who would decide whether to test it?

## Simple qualification criteria

Mark a lead **Qualified** only after a conversation confirms a relevant need, a person responsible for the workflow, and an agreed next step. Record timing and decision process as known or unknown. An attractive company profile alone is not qualification.

For this exercise, move the fictional record to Qualified, add a note clearly marked “practice hypothesis,” and schedule a follow-up. Do not treat that stage change as a real sales-qualified lead.

## Sample email — unsent practice draft

**Subject:** A question about order-status support

Hi Cedar & Loom team,

How do customers find delivery updates today? I'm exploring whether clearer self-service answers could reduce repeated status questions while keeping a person available when needed.

Would a short conversation about your current workflow be useful? If this is not a priority, no problem.

## Sample LinkedIn message — unsent practice draft

Hi, I'm learning how small retail teams handle order-status questions. I'd value your perspective on where customers get stuck and what a useful improvement would look like. Open to a brief conversation?

## What to explain in an interview

Explain the difference between an observed fact and a hypothesis, why the three questions matter, and how the record's next action prevents a conversation from being forgotten. This demonstrates a practice process, not employment or outreach experience.
